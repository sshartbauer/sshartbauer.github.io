Academic Integrity & AI Use

JumpstartGEN is designed to make research more accessible — not to replace the thinking that makes research yours. Here's how to use it with integrity.

What JumpstartGEN Actually Does

JumpstartGEN uses AI to help you structure a research question, generate search strings, surface academic papers via Semantic Scholar, and think through methodology — all starting from a single topic you type in.

It does not write your paper, synthesize sources for you, or produce citable analysis. Every AI-generated suggestion is a starting point: a scaffold, not a finished product.

Key distinction: The papers surfaced by this tool come from Semantic Scholar and are real, peer-reviewed publications. The summaries, methodology suggestions, and framing ideas are AI-generated and must be verified before you rely on them.

What's Okay — and What's Not

Most academic integrity policies focus on whether the work represents your own thinking. Here's how that applies to AI-assisted research tools:

✓ Appropriate Use
  • Using suggested search strings to find papers in databases
  • Browsing surfaced papers and reading the ones that look relevant
  • Using methodology suggestions to understand your options
  • Treating AI-generated framings as brainstorming prompts
  • Downloading the brief to bring to office hours or the library
  • Disclosing AI tool use where required by your institution
✗ Not Appropriate
  • Citing AI-generated summaries as if they came from the papers themselves
  • Submitting AI-generated methodology sections as your own writing
  • Using framing suggestions without reading the actual literature
  • Copying the brief output directly into an assignment
  • Skipping your institution's required disclosure of AI tool use
  • Treating this tool as a replacement for library research instruction

Your Institution's Policy Comes First

Every college and university sets its own rules about AI tool use in academic work. These vary significantly — some prohibit any AI assistance, others require disclosure, and others have no policy yet. It is your responsibility to know your institution's current policy.

Good places to check: your syllabus, your course learning management system, your institution's academic integrity office, and any assignment prompt. When in doubt, ask your instructor before submitting work.

JumpstartGEN makes no representations about whether its use is permitted under any particular institutional policy. This page offers guidance about ethical use — it is not a compliance document.

How to Cite AI Tool Use

If your course allows or requires disclosure of AI tool use, here are example citation formats. Use the format required by your assignment's citation style.

APA 7th Edition

In-text: Research framing and search strategies were developed with assistance from JumpstartGEN (Hartbauer, 2025).

Reference list: Hartbauer, S. (2025). JumpstartGEN [AI research assistance tool]. https://jumpstartgen.vercel.app

MLA 9th Edition

Hartbauer, Seanna. JumpstartGEN. 2025, jumpstartgen.vercel.app. Accessed [date].

Chicago / Turabian

Hartbauer, Seanna. JumpstartGEN (AI research tool), 2025. https://jumpstartgen.vercel.app.
Note on paper citations: Papers surfaced by this tool should be cited from the original source — not from JumpstartGEN. Always locate and read the actual paper before citing it. Use your library's databases to access the full text.

About the AI Behind This Tool

JumpstartGEN uses Claude (Haiku) by Anthropic to generate research scaffolding. AI language models can produce plausible-sounding but incorrect information, especially for specific factual claims, citations, or specialized methodological details.

Always verify AI-generated content against authoritative sources before including it in academic work. This is especially important for: specific statistics or data, claims about what particular scholars argue, descriptions of research designs, and field-specific terminology.

Paper data is sourced from Semantic Scholar, which is a real academic database maintained by the Allen Institute for AI. Paper titles, abstracts, authors, and citations come from Semantic Scholar and are generally reliable — but you should still access and read the original sources.
