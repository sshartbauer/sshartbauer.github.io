📊 Source Tier Guide

Click "Show guide" above to expand a ranked breakdown of source credibility. Use it to evaluate which sources are strong enough to cite in your paper.

1
Primary / Raw Data Original datasets you analyze yourself: government surveys, health registries, census data (e.g. NHANES, IPUMS, Census.gov). Highest credibility for quantitative work.
2
Peer-Reviewed Research Published studies vetted by experts in the field, and the gold standard for citation. Prioritize primary research articles over review articles when possible.
3
Government & Institutional Reports Reliable secondary sources like CDC reports, WHO white papers, and Congressional Budget Office analyses. Good for context; not always peer-reviewed.
4
News & General Web Useful for understanding the public conversation, but not for citation in academic work. Always trace news stories back to their primary sources.
