📊 Source Tier Guide

1
Primary / Raw Data Original datasets you analyze yourself — government surveys, health registries, census data (e.g. NHANES, IPUMS, Census.gov). Highest credibility for quantitative work.
2
Peer-Reviewed Research Published studies vetted by experts in the field — the gold standard for citation. Prioritize primary research articles over review articles when possible.
3
Government & Institutional Reports Reliable secondary sources — CDC reports, WHO white papers, Congressional Budget Office analyses. Good for context; not always peer-reviewed.
4
News & General Web Useful for understanding the public conversation — not for citation in academic work. Always trace news stories back to their primary sources.
