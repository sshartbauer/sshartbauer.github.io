How I approach research, data sourcing, and the role of AI tools in my work

The Way I Use AI Responsibly

Every paper and project on this site is my own analysis. Where AI tools are used, they are used as research scaffolding, not as a replacement for reading, thinking, or writing. I treat AI output the way I treat a conversation with a librarian: useful for direction, but never a primary source.

Data always comes from named, citable sources. I do not present AI-generated content as factual without independent verification. All methods and data provenance for research papers are documented within the work itself.

JumpstartGEN and other tools on this site are designed to give students a structured head start. They are not intended to do any thinking; simply to provide the substrate for a user's own thoughts. Every tool includes its own guidance on appropriate use.

"But you didn't build these tools!"
My foray into the world of HTML and Java more than 20 years ago has helped me edit the code, but no, I did not build the tools myself. I did, however, spend dozens (in some cases, near hundreds) of hours dictating what they should do, how they should look, what features they should have, and countless other parameters that make them useful for their intended audience of first-generation college students like myself. I do not claim to have built them, but I do claim ownership and responsibility over them as they are of my own conception and design.

Moreover, I built these tools for myself. I did not originally intend to share them. I'm showcasing them here for several different reasons, chief among them that I wanted to share them with my classmates who have struggled so intensely beside me. I don't make money off of these; I don't charge for their use, and I am open and honest about their origins.

So why the big defense? Because given the controversy AI has become, my sole intention for choosing this medium for my academic portfolio was to literally display how AI can be used to further an academic career without direct exploitation. Is AI good or bad? Are the resources it uses too numerous? Am I woefully ignorant for using it this extensively? These are all questions I am not only aware of, but I tend to obsess over. I maintain active subscriptions with daily email digests to stay current on the latest research around each.

Appropriate Use

AI tools are most useful when they help you find and evaluate real sources, not when they generate the substance of your argument. Here's an actual list of what I consider to be appropriate:

What I do
  • Use AI to generate search strings and explore what literature exists
  • Read and cite actual papers, not AI summaries of them
  • Verify every factual claim against a primary or peer-reviewed source
  • Disclose AI tool use wherever required by my institution
  • Treat AI methodology suggestions as starting points to evaluate, not conclusions to accept
What I don't do
  • Cite AI-generated content as if it came from a source
  • Submit AI-written analysis as my own work
  • Use AI output as a substitute for reading the literature
  • Present AI-generated statistics or claims without verification
  • Build tools that obscure how they work or encourage misuse

About the AI

JumpstartGEN uses Claude Haiku by Anthropic for research search strings and general optimization. Paper data is sourced from Semantic Scholar, maintained by the Allen Institute for AI. Paper titles, abstracts, authors, and citations come directly from their API and are not AI-generated.

AI language models can produce information that sounds factual, but is in reality incorrect. This is especially true for specific factual claims or specialized methodological details. Any AI-generated output on this site or in these tools should be independently verified before use in academic work.

You might wonder then, why would I use AI if I just have to go back and verify anyway? Responsible use of AI will give you a good head start, but you will still have to do the work. Reframing "AI works for me" into "AI simplifies for me" in your own mindset is a good place to start.
