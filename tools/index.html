What I Actually Did

These tools were built with significant AI assistance — I'm transparent about that on my AI policy page. But AI doesn't have clinical experience, organic chemistry coursework, or a reason to care whether a first-gen student can parse a research question at midnight. Here's what I brought to each of these.

Clinical Knowledge & Language
  • All phrase content drawn from real patient interaction at Martinez Regional Center
  • Chose which situations actually come up most in med-surg (not a textbook list)
  • Validated informal tú register as appropriate for clinical context
  • Identified pronunciation pain points from my own Spanish learning experience
  • Ongoing: testing phrases in actual patient encounters and revising
Organic Chemistry Content
  • Selected all reactions, functional groups, and mechanisms from real coursework
  • Curated distractor answers that reflect actual common student mistakes
  • Wrote mechanism walkthroughs and wrong-answer explanations from a student POV
  • Aligned chapter structure to OpenStax OChem so it maps to class
  • Tested every card against my own gaps and revised accordingly
Game Design & Pedagogy
  • Designed the reaction tree concept — work through all reactions a substrate can do
  • Chose electron currency mechanic to make correct answers feel meaningful
  • Specified the hint tiers based on what I actually wish I had while studying
  • Decided to rotate missing piece (product / reagent / substrate) to prevent pattern-matching shortcuts
  • Iterative testing every build as a real student under real exam pressure
Research & Portfolio
  • All research questions formulated from genuine clinical observations
  • Dataset selection and sourcing (CA HCAI, CMS, NHANES, SEER, BRFSS, CDC)
  • All data cleaning and analysis in R once underway
  • JumpstartGEN's framing born from watching peers struggle with their first papers
  • Every word on this site written, edited, and revised by me
